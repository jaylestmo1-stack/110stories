# 110 Stories: A Collection of Memories 📖

A curated collection of personal narratives related to the World Trade Center and the events of September 11, 2001. Based on the play by **Sarah Tuft**, this repository serves as a digital archive for the "110 Stories" project, preserving voices and memories for future generations.

---

## ⚠️ Content Warning

This collection contains firsthand accounts of the events of September 11, 2001. The narratives include descriptions of a traumatic historical event that may be distressing for some readers. We encourage readers to approach this material with care.

### 🔍 Themes & Perspectives
To provide a "scent of information" before you download, this collection includes firsthand testimonials from:
- **First Responders & Recovery Workers:** Firefighters, police officers, ironworkers, and K9 handlers.
- **Caregivers & Support:** Nurses, doctors, chaplains, and chiropractors.
- **Witnesses & Survivors:** Photojournalists, office workers, and everyday New Yorkers.
- **Historical Preservation:** A commitment to keeping these diverse voices alive for future generations.

---

## 💡 About this Collection

"110 Stories" is a tribute to the human experience surrounding the World Trade Center. It brings together a diverse array of perspectives, from everyday moments in the shadow of the towers to the profound impact of 9/11. By documenting these stories, we ensure that the personal history of this landmark and its community is never forgotten.

## 📖 Tips for Reading

To get the most out of this collection, we recommend:
- **Using a PDF viewer with search:** Most modern browsers and PDF readers allow you to search for keywords (e.g., `Ctrl+F` or `Cmd+F`) to find specific topics or locations mentioned in the stories.
- **Utilizing Bookmarks:** If your PDF viewer supports it, look for the Table of Contents or Bookmarks sidebar to navigate between stories more easily.
- **Setting Aside Time:** These stories are personal and evocative; we recommend reading them in a quiet space where you can reflect on the narratives.
